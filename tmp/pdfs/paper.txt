1. Introduction
Blockchain technology has quickly evolved into atrans-
formative paradigm across multiple domains, providing de-
centralized, tamper-resistant, and transparent solutions for
data exchange and transaction processing [1],[2],[3],[4]. Its
fundamental properties, which include immutability, trace-
ability [5],[6], and consensus-driven trust [7], have enabled
widespreadadoptioninfieldssuchasdigitalfinance[8],[9],
supply chain management [10],[11], healthcare [12], the
InternetofThings(IoT)[13],[14],[15],[16],andsmartgrids
[17],[18],[19],[20].
Despitethesebenefits,blockchainsystemsstillfacesig-
nificant performance bottlenecks, particularly in terms of
scalability [21],[22],[23]. Existing public blockchains, such
as Bitcoin and Ethereum, have limited transaction process-
ing capabilities, averaging around 7 and 30 transactions per
second (TPS), respectively [24],[25]. These rates fall far
short of the requirements for high-throughput applications,
suchasfinancialexchangesandIoTnetworks[26],[27],[28].
Inaddition,thelatencyrequiredfortransactionconfirmation
canrangefromafewsecondstominutes,makingblockchain
unsuitable for latency-sensitive services like real-time pay-
ments or industrial automation [29],[30],[31]. These lim-
itations are exacerbated by dynamic workloads, in which
transaction arrival rates and network conditions (such as
node participation and network latency) fluctuate unpre-
dictablyasaresultofexternaluserbehaviororsystem-level
events. In such cases, traditional blockchain configurations,
which are statically defined at the time of deployment, lack
the responsiveness required to adapt to real-time changes.

2.2. Blockchainsharding
Sharding techniques partition the blockchain network
into multiple shards to enhance parallelism and scalability.
Cai et al. [46] developed Benzene, a collaboration-based
shardingsystemthatimprovesthroughputwhilemaintaining
shard-level security. Li et al. [43] presented LBChain, a
load-balancing sharding scheme that dynamically migrates
accounts among shards based on activity levels, effectively
balancing transaction loads and boosting throughput. Yang
et al. [47] introduced EZchain, a passive sharding protocol
that achieves constant communication and storage costs
withoutrequiringcross-shardtransactionsorbeaconchains,
offeringimprovedscalabilityandsecurityinlargenetworks.
Liu et al. [48] proposed a flexible sharding (FS) blockchain
protocol that features cross-shard Byzantine fault tolerance
(CSBFT) to reduce confirmation delays, multiple parallel
CSBFT to enhance system parallelism, and decentralized
shard reconfiguration. This effectively addresses key chal-
lenges in cross-shard transaction processing and security
vulnerabilities. Jia et al. [49] proposed a low cross-shard
protocol, which features a multi-level state model and a
community overlap propagation algorithm to dramatically
reduce cross-shard transactions. The X-shard system [50]
addresses cross-shard processing bottlenecks through trans-
action pattern-based allocation and optimistic parallel pro-
cessing, achieving near-linear throughput scaling and re-
duced latency.

Thissectionidentifieskeylimitationsincurrentblockchain

3.1. Disjoint optimizationand lack ofadaptability
indynamic environments
Current blockchain optimization strategies often treat
transaction workload prediction and system-level configu-
ration separately, and many of them use static configura-
tions or rule-based policies, assuming stable environments
[38],[39],[40],[41],[42],[51]. In dynamic blockchain envi-
ronments, transaction arrival rates fluctuate unpredictably
due to user behavior or external events, and node partic-
ipation and transaction volumes are also volatile. Without
tightintegrationbetweenpredictionandcontrol,andwithout
adaptivestrategiesthatcanautonomouslyupdateconfigura-
tionsinresponsetoobservedchanges,workloadpredictions
cannot effectively guide proactive adjustments to system
parameterslikeblocksizeorgaslimit,leadingtoinefficient
resourceallocation,increasedlatency,andpoorperformance
under shiftingworkloads.
3.2. Limitedgeneralization under data constraints
Machine learning–based blockchain performance pre-
diction often relies on abundant, high-quality training data,
which is rarely available in real-world deployments. Public
blockchains may have imbalanced datasets, while consor-
tium blockchains often suffer from small sample sizes [52].
Additionally, blockchain data is frequently noisy due to
inconsistent node behaviors or heterogeneous network con-
ditions,challenginglearningalgorithmsandlimitingmodel
generalization [53]. A robust framework must incorporate
modelsresilienttosuchdata deficiencies.

4. System model
4.1. Overallframework
Figure 1 depicts the proposed ChainOptNet framework
for blockchain performance optimization, which dynam-
ically adjusts the block size according to real-time net-
work conditions and historical transaction information. The
framework takes historical transaction arrival rates together
with blockchain configuration parameters, including block
size,thenumberoforderingnodes,latency,andthroughput,
as inputs, and outputs the optimal block size to improve
overall blockchain performance. As illustrated in Fig. 1,
the proposed framework consists of the following three
components:
(1) Transaction arrival rate prediction: The Convolu-
tionalFeatureAggregationandDistribution(CFAD)model
predictsfuturetransactionarrivalratesfromhistoricaltrans-
action data by effectively capturing temporal dependencies
and local fluctuation patterns.
(2) Blockchain performance prediction: A multi-model
collaborative prediction framework integrates seven ma-
chinelearningmodels,includingLinearRegression,Bayesian
RidgeRegression,DecisionTreeRegressor,SupportVector
Regression, Gradient Boosting Regressor, Random Forest
Regressor, and eXtreme Gradient Boosting Regressor, to
estimate blockchain performance metrics such as latency
and throughput. The prediction results are further fused
using an inverse error-weighted ensemble strategy, thereby
improvingprediction robustnessandaccuracy.
(3) Blockchain performance optimization: A Dynamic
Weight Deep Q-Network (DW-DQN) dynamically deter-
mines the optimal block size. Specifically, the optimiza-
tion module consists of a DQN-based block-size controller
and a trend-aware reward weighting module. The DQN
learns the optimal block-size adjustment policy, while the
reward weighting module adaptively computes the relative
importance of latency and throughput according to recent
performance trends, enabling adaptive multi-objective opti-
mization.

5.1. Experimental settings
5.1.1. Experimental design
We conducted three sets of experimentss: (1) predicting
transaction arrival rates, (2) predicting blockchain perfor-
mance through inverse error ensemble learning, and (3)
optimizing blockchain performance.
5.1.2. Datasets
In Experiment 1 used an open-source dataset of park-
ing lot records collected in real-world scenarios to predict
transaction arrival rates. Although originally designed for

latency, and throughput. These parameters were applied
to the blockchain performance prediction and optimization
tasks, respectively.
5.1.3. Comparable methods
To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the
proposed ChainOptNet framework, representative baseline
methods wereselected for each task.
For transaction arrival rate prediction, we compared the
proposed CFAD model with several representative time-
series forecasting models, including Autoformer, Informer,
Transformer,LSTM,GRU,LightTS,MLP,andablockchain-
oriented temporal prediction baseline [42]. These meth-
ods cover recurrent neural networks, Transformer-based
architectures, lightweight forecasting models, and domain-
specific predictionmethods.
For blockchain performance prediction, the proposed
multi-model collaborative prediction framework was com-
pared with several widely used regression models, includ-
ing Linear Regression (LR), Bayesian Ridge Regression
(BR), Bayesian Regularization (BYR), Decision Tree Re-
gression(DTR),GradientBoostingRegression(GBR),Ran-
domForestRegression(RFR),andeXtremeGradientBoost-
ing (XGB). These models represent linear regression, prob-
abilistic regression, decision tree learning, and ensemble
learning methods that are widely adopted for blockchain
performance prediction.
Forblockchainperformanceoptimization,wecompared
the proposed DW-DQN with representative non-RL op-
timization settings, including fixed block-size configura-
tions and a prediction-driven score-based adjustment strat-
egy [42]. Such methods adjust parameters according to
predefined performance scores or static configuration rules,
whereas DW-DQN formulates block-size optimization as
a reinforcement learning problem and dynamically adjusts
reward weights according to network conditions, enabling
more adaptive optimization under varying workloads.
Toensurefairnessandreproducibility,allbaselinemeth-
ods were evaluated using identical datasets, preprocessing
procedures,experimentalsettings, and evaluation metrics.

computational efficiency.

6. Conclusion
2 0 0 0 2 0 4 0 6 0 8 0 1 0 0 1 2 0 1 4 0 1 6 0 1 8 0 2 0 0
This paper introduces ChainOptNet, a comprehensive
blockchainperformanceoptimizationframeworkthatachieves
significant system improvements by leveraging three key
technical innovations. First, for transaction arrival rate pre-
diction,weintroducetheConvolutionalFeatureAggregation
and Distribution (CFAD) model, which leverages a novel
deep learning architecture to effectively capture temporal
dependencies and complex distributional patterns in trans-
action data. Second, in the domain of performance predic-
tion, a multi-model collaborative framework is proposed to
improve predictive accuracy and robustness by intelligently
combining heterogeneous machine learning models. Third,
the Dynamic Weight Deep Q-Network (DW-DQN) serves
as the optimization mechanism, combining a DQN-based
block-size controller with a trend-aware dynamic reward
weighting module to support real-time adaptive block-size
adjustment and balanced optimization across multiple ob-
jectives.
Extensive experimental evaluations demonstrate the ef-
fectivenessoftheproposedframework.Theresultsshowthat
ChainOptNet improves latency reduction and throughput
enhancement compared with representative forecasting, re-
gression,ensemble-learning,fixed-configuration,andscore-
based optimization baselines, while maintaining high op-
timization efficiency across varying transaction loads. The
framework has a high degree of generalization and adapt-
ability,makingitsuitableforuseinawiderangeofdynamic
blockchain environments.
Future research will investigate several promising di-
rectionstoimproveChainOptNet’sapplicabilityandperfor-
mance. These include: (1) lightweight optimization algo-
rithms to reduce computational overhead, (2) extending the
frameworktosupportcross-chainperformanceoptimization
in heterogeneous public chain ecosystems, (3) designing
scenario-specific optimization strategies tailored to particu-
larapplicationdomains,(4)incorporatingadvancedsecurity
andprivacymechanismsduringoptimizationprocesses,and
(5) improving system adaptability. These future efforts are
