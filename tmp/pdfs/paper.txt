Blockchain has emerged as a transformative technology with numerous applications due to its tamper-
proof, traceable, and decentralized architecture. Nonetheless, its performance remains constrained,
particularly in areas such as latency and throughput, which pose significant barriers to large-scale
deployment. This paper proposes ChainOptNet, a novel blockchain system performance optimization
framework that addresses these challenges with three key technical contributions. First, we develop
the Convolutional Feature Aggregation and Distribution (CFAD) model to forecast transaction arrival
rates. This model uses a deep learning architecture to capture transaction data’s temporal dependencies
and complex statistical patterns. Second, we present a multi-model collaborative framework for
blockchain performance prediction that improves predictive accuracy and robustness by intelligently
integrating diverse machine learning models. Third, we create a Dynamic Weight Deep Q-Network
(DW-DQN) optimization mechanism that combines a DQN-based block-size controller with a trend-
aware dynamic reward weighting module for balanced multi-objective optimization. Experimental
evaluations show that CFAD achieves the lowest MAE and RMSE at all three evaluated time
intervals, while the performance prediction ensemble achieves the lowest MAPE for both latency and
throughput. The optimization module also reduces average latency and increases average throughput
relative to the evaluated fixed block-size configurations. The reported latency optimization rate reaches
50.73% relative to a fixed block size of 100, while the reported throughput optimization rate reaches
22.09% relative to a fixed block size of 50. These results demonstrate the framework’s scalability,
adaptability, and strong potential for practical deployment in diverse blockchain environments.
1. Introduction
Blockchain technology has quickly evolved into a trans-
formative paradigm across multiple domains, providing de-
centralized, tamper-resistant, and transparent solutions for
data exchange and transaction processing [1],[2],[3],[4]. Its
fundamental properties, which include immutability, trace-
ability [5],[6], and consensus-driven trust [7], have enabled
widespread adoption in fields such as digital finance [8],[9],
supply chain management [10],[11], healthcare [12], the
Internet of Things (IoT) [13],[14],[15],[16], and smart grids
[17],[18],[19],[20].
Despite these benefits, blockchain systems still face sig-
nificant performance bottlenecks, particularly in terms of
scalability [21],[22],[23]. Existing public blockchains, such
as Bitcoin and Ethereum, have limited transaction process-
ing capabilities, averaging around 7 and 30 transactions per
second (TPS), respectively [24],[25]. These rates fall far
short of the requirements for high-throughput applications,
such as financial exchanges and IoT networks [26],[27],[28].
In addition, the latency required for transaction confirmation
can range from a few seconds to minutes, making blockchain
unsuitable for latency-sensitive services like real-time pay-
ments or industrial automation [29],[30],[31]. These lim-
itations are exacerbated by dynamic workloads, in which
transaction arrival rates and network conditions (such as
node participation and network latency) fluctuate unpre-
dictably as a result of external user behavior or system-level
events. In such cases, traditional blockchain configurations,
which are statically defined at the time of deployment, lack
the responsiveness required to adapt to real-time changes.

by fluctuating node participation, heterogeneous hardware

while maintaining stable performance under changing net-

To address these performance issues, researchers have
proposed a number of improvement strategies. Alternative
consensus algorithms such as Proof-of-Stake (PoS) and
Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT), aim to reduce
consensus latency and increase throughput [32],[33],[34],[35],
but they frequently compromise decentralization and scala-
bility. Some approaches use heuristic or rule-based tuning
of system parameters, such as adjusting block size or gas
limits based on network statistics [36],[37],[38],[39],[40].
Some researchers have also introduced machine learning-
based methods for tuning blockchain parameters [41], with
the goal of optimizing performance in a more adaptive and
data-driven manner. Recently, some researchers have used
performance scoring mechanisms to dynamically optimize
blockchain configuration parameters [42]. These methods
can eﬀectively supplement and synergize with existing
eﬀorts, providing a promising path to improving blockchain
performance.
However, these strategies typically lack adaptability and
robustness because they rely on manually defined thresholds
that may be insuﬃcient under unpredictable or changing
conditions. Furthermore, while machine learning methods

by systematically coordinating learning-based components.
Extensive evaluations across a variety of blockchain sce-

• We introduce CFAD, a convolutional sequence model

environments.

blockchain scenarios to validate ChainOptNet’s supe-
rior performance, adaptability, and scalability under
varying workloads and network conditions. The find-
ings show that ChainOptNet can learn eﬀective pa-
rameter adjustment strategies by leveraging temporal
patterns, ensemble learning, and dynamic reward tun-
ing, demonstrating ChainOptNet’s eﬀectiveness and
superiority for blockchain system performance opti-
mization.

2. Related work
As blockchain technology becomes increasingly widespread
across various applications, performance issues such as
low throughput and high latency have emerged as major
bottlenecks. Existing research has explored a range of op-
timization strategies from diﬀerent perspectives to address
these challenges.
Recent studies have also explored transaction conflict
management and ledger design. Wu et al. [44] proposed Fab-
ricTCA, which improves Hyperledger Fabric throughput by
mitigating intra-block and inter-block transaction conflicts.
Ma et al. [45] introduced MixChain, an Account-Vessel
hybrid ledger model that reduces transaction dependencies
to facilitate parallel execution. These approaches improve
blockchain performance through changes to transaction pro-
cessing and ledger architecture, whereas ChainOptNet fo-
cuses on adaptive block-size adjustment.
2.1. Improvement of consensus algorithms
Improving the consensus mechanism is one of the most
common approaches to enhancing blockchain performance.
Gao et al. [33] proposed a piecewise hash graph consensus
algorithm designed for industrial IoT, which significantly
improves throughput and reduces latency. Zhang et al. [34]
enhanced the performance of Hyperledger Fabric by modi-
fying its consensus module, thereby achieving a better trade-
oﬀ between security and eﬃciency. Xu et al. [32] introduced
AdaptChain, an elastic blockchain that supports adaptive
expansion and contraction based on transaction volume,
optimizing resource utilization while maintaining high per-
formance. Li et al. [46] proposed a hybrid consensus mecha-
nism that integrates intra-shard processing with lightweight

global coordination, addressing the issues of vulnerable
consensus security and delayed atomicity in cross-shard
transactions while maintaining adaptive shard management.
Islam et al. [47] proposed Proof of Random Leader (PoRL), a

selection, thus addressing the limitations of low throughput

2.2. Blockchain sharding
Sharding techniques partition the blockchain network
into multiple shards to enhance parallelism and scalability.
Cai et al. [48] developed Benzene, a collaboration-based
sharding system that improves throughput while maintaining
shard-level security. Li et al. [43] presented LBChain, a
load-balancing sharding scheme that dynamically migrates
accounts among shards based on activity levels, eﬀectively
balancing transaction loads and boosting throughput. Yang
et al. [49] introduced EZchain, a passive sharding protocol
that achieves constant communication and storage costs
without requiring cross-shard transactions or beacon chains,
oﬀering improved scalability and security in large networks.
Liu et al. [50] proposed a flexible sharding (FS) blockchain
protocol that features cross-shard Byzantine fault tolerance
(CSBFT) to reduce confirmation delays, multiple parallel
CSBFT to enhance system parallelism, and decentralized
shard reconfiguration. This eﬀectively addresses key chal-
lenges in cross-shard transaction processing and security
vulnerabilities. Jia et al. [51] proposed a low cross-shard
protocol, which features a multi-level state model and a
community overlap propagation algorithm to dramatically
reduce cross-shard transactions. The X-shard system [52]
addresses cross-shard processing bottlenecks through trans-
action pattern-based allocation and optimistic parallel pro-
cessing, achieving near-linear throughput scaling and re-
duced latency.
2.3. Optimization based on traditional algorithms
Several studies have employed classical optimization
algorithms to fine-tune blockchain parameters. Jamil et al.
[38] applied Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to evaluate
the trade-oﬀ between throughput and latency in Hyperledger
Fabric, aiming to determine the optimal transaction arrival
rate. Chen et al. [39] deployed a private Ethereum IoT
network and used probabilistic fitting to model its latency
performance. Wilhelmi et al. [40] utilized Markov Chains to
determine the optimal block size configuration for minimiz-
ing latency through numerical analysis.
2.4. Machine learning-based optimization
Recent advances have introduced machine learning (ML)
to predict system behavior and support intelligent decision-
making in blockchain environments. Wang et al. [41] pro-
posed a performance prediction framework that employs
Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) and Long Short-Term Memory
(LSTM) networks to predict blockchain performance and
transaction arrival rates. However, this approach does not
address issues such as the cost of collecting blockchain
performance data, its generalization capability under small
sample sizes, and the integration of predictive modeling
with system-level optimization. Other prediction-driven op-
timization methods combine temporal workload forecasting,
performance prediction, and score-based configuration ad-
justment to improve throughput and latency [42]. Although
these methods provide a more flexible alternative to manual
tuning, they often depend on predefined scoring functions
or fixed decision rules, making it diﬃcult to adapt the op-
timization objective when latency and throughput priorities
change across operating regimes.
Reinforcement learning has also been applied to permis-
sioned blockchain optimization. Tang et al. [53] proposed
Rail-Fabric, a framework for low-latency movement author-
ity generation in train control systems. The framework uses a
Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) to dynamically partition
the railway network and adjust sub-blockchain boundaries.
While Rail-Fabric focuses on topology-aware partitioning,
ChainOptNet combines workload forecasting and perfor-
mance prediction with block-size control and trend-aware
reward weighting.
3. Research motivation
This section identifies key limitations in current blockchain
optimization: the disjoint handling of workload prediction
and system tuning, the poor generalization of machine
learning models under data scarcity and noise, and the lack
of adaptability and real-time responsiveness in dynamic
environments.
3.1. Disjoint optimization and lack of adaptability
in dynamic environments
Current blockchain optimization strategies often treat
transaction workload prediction and system-level configu-
ration separately, and many of them use static configura-
tions or rule-based policies, assuming stable environments
[38],[39],[40],[41],[42],[54]. In dynamic blockchain envi-
ronments, transaction arrival rates fluctuate unpredictably
due to user behavior or external events, and node partic-
ipation and transaction volumes are also volatile. Without
tight integration between prediction and control, and without
adaptive strategies that can autonomously update configura-
tions in response to observed changes, workload predictions
cannot eﬀectively guide proactive adjustments to system
parameters like block size or gas limit, leading to ineﬃcient
resource allocation, increased latency, and poor performance
under shifting workloads.
3.2. Limited generalization under data constraints
Machine learning–based blockchain performance pre-
diction often relies on abundant, high-quality training data,
which is rarely available in real-world deployments. Public

3.3. Summary
To address these gaps, this paper proposes ChainOptNet,

4. System model
4.1. Overall framework
Figure 1 depicts the proposed ChainOptNet framework
for blockchain performance optimization, which dynam-
ically adjusts the block size according to real-time net-
work conditions and historical transaction information. The
framework takes historical transaction arrival rates together
with blockchain configuration parameters, including block
size, the number of ordering nodes, latency, and throughput,
as inputs, and outputs the optimal block size to improve
overall blockchain performance. As illustrated in Fig. 1,
the proposed framework consists of the following three
components:
(1) Transaction arrival rate prediction: The Convolu-
tional Feature Aggregation and Distribution (CFAD) model
predicts future transaction arrival rates from historical trans-
action data by eﬀectively capturing temporal dependencies
and local fluctuation patterns.
(2) Blockchain performance prediction: A multi-model
collaborative prediction framework integrates seven ma-
chine learning models, including Linear Regression, Bayesian
Ridge Regression, Decision Tree Regressor, Support Vector
Regression, Gradient Boosting Regressor, Random Forest
Regressor, and eXtreme Gradient Boosting Regressor, to
estimate blockchain performance metrics such as latency
and throughput. The prediction results are further fused
using an inverse error-weighted ensemble strategy, thereby
improving prediction robustness and accuracy.
(3) Blockchain performance optimization: A Dynamic
Weight Deep Q-Network (DW-DQN) dynamically deter-
mines the optimal block size. Specifically, the optimiza-
tion module consists of a DQN-based block-size controller
and a trend-aware reward weighting module. The DQN
learns the optimal block-size adjustment policy, while the
reward weighting module adaptively computes the relative
importance of latency and throughput according to recent
performance trends, enabling adaptive multi-objective opti-
mization.

Inspired by the recently proposed SOFTS model [57],
which demonstrates eﬃcient and scalable performance in
multivariate time series forecasting via its centralized STAR
(STar Aggregate-Redistribute) module, we create a modified
version specifically for predicting blockchain transaction

system performance. As shown in Figure 2, the CFAD

the data. By using a convolutional layer with a kernel size
of 1 and ’same’ padding, CFAD eﬃciently preserves the

4.3. Blockchain performance prediction model
To address the limitations of single machine learning
models in capturing complex interactions among features,
this study proposes a multi-model collaborative prediction
framework that combines seven diﬀerent machine learn-
ing algorithms. This framework enhances predictive accu-
racy and adaptability in volatile blockchain environments by
leveraging model diversity and dynamic weighting.
The proposed framework integrates the following base
regressors: Linear Regression (LR), Bayesian Ridge Re-
gression (BYR), Decision Tree Regressor (DTR), Bagging
Regressor (BR), Gradient Boosting Regressor (GBR), Ran-
dom Forest Regressor (RFR), and eXtreme Gradient Boost-
ing (XGB). These models cover both parametric and non-
parametric domains, allowing the ensemble to capture a
wide range of data characteristics, including linear depen-
dencies and complex nonlinear patterns.

multi-model framework. It is divided into two major stages:
(i) an oﬄine training phase, in which base learners are
trained on enriched features and weighted using inverse-
error-based softmax; and (ii) an online prediction phase, in
which model weights are dynamically updated using a slid-
ing window of recent observations to maintain adaptability
under changing conditions.

4.4. Blockchain performance optimization with
DW-DQN
The proposed Dynamic Weight Deep Q-Network (DW-
DQN) framework is intended to address the limitations
of traditional reinforcement learning approaches in multi-
objective optimization tasks within blockchain environ-
ments, particularly in the dynamic adjustment of block size
to optimize throughput and latency. Unlike traditional Deep
Q-Networks (DQNs), which use a static reward formulation,
DW-DQN combines a DQN-based block-size controller
with a trend-aware dynamic reward weighting module. The
DQN learns the action policy, while the weighting module
deterministically updates the latency and throughput weights
according to recent system performance trends.

5.2. The performance of ChainOptNet
5.2.1. Prediction performance on transaction arrival
rate
To evaluate the generalization capability and robust-
ness of the proposed Convolutional Feature Aggregation
and Distribution (CFAD) model in predicting transaction
arrival rates, experiments were conducted at three temporal
granularities: 1-minute, 2-minute, and 5-minute intervals.
As shown in Figure 5 and Table 1, CFAD achieves the
lowest MAE and RMSE at all three evaluated time intervals
and the lowest MAPE at the 2-minute and 5-minute inter-
vals. At the 1-minute interval, Autoformer achieves a lower
MAPE (0.2220) than CFAD (0.2486). CFAD’s MAE and
RMSE results are attributed to its global one-dimensional
convolution-based sequence encoder, which preserves the
inherent sequential structure of transaction data, allowing
for better capture of fine-grained temporal dependencies and
improved responsiveness to sudden fluctuations.
In the 5-minute prediction scenario, CFAD achieved
significant improvements in MAE, RMSE, and MAPE com-
pared with representative temporal forecasting and blockchain-
oriented prediction baselines. Specifically, Table 1 reports
that, at the 5-minute interval, CFAD reduces MAE from
0.0293 to 0.0247, RMSE from 0.0387 to 0.0325, and MAPE
from 0.1085 to 0.0887 compared with the prior blockchain-
oriented prediction baseline.

5.2.3. Performance of the optimization mechanism
with DW-DQN
This section evaluates the proposed blockchain per-
formance optimization mechanism based on the Dynamic
Weight Deep Q-Network (DW-DQN), focusing on improve-
ments in system latency and throughput. Experiments were
conducted with four initial block sizes (50, 100, 150, and
200) under various transaction loads (TPS). The results
demonstrate DW-DQN’s adaptability and robustness in
solving multi-objective optimization tasks.
DW-DQN consistently enhances system performance
across various TPS levels, with notable improvements ob-
served under high-load conditions. For example, when the
initial block size was set to 150 and TPS increased from
60 to 140, the latency before optimization reached approx-
imately 1.25 seconds. In contrast, DW-DQN maintained
latency between 0.25 and 0.9 seconds, achieving an average
improvement of 54% (Figure 7).
In terms of throughput, DW-DQN outperforms fixed
block size baselines, especially under high TPS conditions.
For example, at TPS = 160, DW-DQN achieved a maximum
throughput 30% higher than the static approach (Figure 8).
Tables 3 and 4 summarize the optimization rates for
latency and throughput, respectively. DW-DQN significantly
reduced average latency and increased throughput across
all initial block sizes. For example, with an initial block
size of 200, latency was reduced from 0.93 seconds to 0.43
seconds (50.09% improvement), and throughput increased
from 92.37 TPS to 103.09 TPS (11.35% improvement).

5.4. Hyperparameter analysis
To investigate the sensitivity of the proposed optimiza-
tion framework to key DW-DQN hyperparameters, we con-
ducted a comprehensive sensitivity analysis by varying the
discount factor and replay buﬀer size on the HFBDP dataset.
The corresponding latency and throughput obtained under
diﬀerent parameter settings are summarized in Table 6,
while the variation trends are illustrated in Fig. 9.
Discount factor (𝛾). The discount factor determines the
importance of future rewards during policy learning. As
shown in Table 6, the optimization performance improves
steadily as 𝛾 increases from 0.80 to 0.99. Specifically, the
average latency decreases from 0.56 s to 0.43 s, while the
average throughput increases from 92.34 TPS to 103.09
TPS. When 𝛾 is relatively small, the agent mainly focuses
on immediate rewards, resulting in conservative block-size
adjustment decisions and relatively lower optimization per-
formance. As 𝛾 increases beyond 0.93, the agent gradu-
ally learns more eﬀective long-term optimization policies,
leading to noticeable reductions in latency and continuous
improvements in throughput. The best optimization perfor-
mance is achieved when 𝛾 = 0.99, indicating that emphasiz-
ing long-term cumulative rewards is beneficial for dynamic
blockchain parameter optimization.
Replay buﬀer size. The replay buﬀer size controls the
number of historical experiences used for network updates.
As the replay buﬀer size increases from 1000 to 10000,
both latency and throughput are continuously improved.
In particular, the average latency decreases from 0.54 s to
0.43 s, while the throughput increases from 94.12 TPS to
103.09 TPS. This improvement demonstrates that a larger re-
play buﬀer provides more diverse training samples, thereby
improving policy stability and convergence. However, when
the replay buﬀer size exceeds 10000, the performance gain
becomes marginal. Although the replay buﬀer continues
to increase to 12000, 15000, and 20000, the throughput
remains almost unchanged while the latency exhibits a slight
increase. This observation suggests that excessively large
replay memories introduce additional storage overhead with-
out providing significant improvements in optimization per-
formance.
Overall, the proposed DW-DQN framework demon-
strates stable optimization performance under diﬀerent hy-
perparameter settings. Both the discount factor and replay
buﬀer size exhibit clear convergence trends, indicating that
the proposed optimization strategy is relatively robust to
hyperparameter variations. Based on the above analysis, a
discount factor of 0.99 and a replay buﬀer size of 10000
are selected for all subsequent experiments, as they provide
the best trade-oﬀ between optimization eﬀectiveness and
computational eﬃciency.

6. Conclusion
This paper introduces ChainOptNet, a comprehensive
blockchain performance optimization framework that achieves
significant system improvements by leveraging three key
technical innovations. First, for transaction arrival rate pre-
diction, we introduce the Convolutional Feature Aggregation
and Distribution (CFAD) model, which leverages a novel
deep learning architecture to eﬀectively capture temporal
dependencies and complex distributional patterns in trans-
action data. Second, in the domain of performance predic-
tion, a multi-model collaborative framework is proposed to
improve predictive accuracy and robustness by intelligently
combining heterogeneous machine learning models. Third,
the Dynamic Weight Deep Q-Network (DW-DQN) serves
as the optimization mechanism, combining a DQN-based
block-size controller with a trend-aware dynamic reward
weighting module to support real-time adaptive block-size
adjustment and balanced optimization across multiple ob-
jectives.
Extensive experimental evaluations demonstrate the ef-
fectiveness of the proposed framework. The results show that
ChainOptNet improves latency reduction and throughput
enhancement compared with representative forecasting, re-
gression, ensemble-learning, fixed-configuration, and score-
based optimization baselines, while maintaining high op-
timization eﬃciency across varying transaction loads. The
